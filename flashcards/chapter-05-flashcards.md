## Chapter 5: Defining Data Needs and Mapping Data Sources — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Data Steward vs Data Owner — key difference? | Owner = accountable for business use/access authorization; Steward = operational custodian of definitions, quality, and metadata. Exam tip: identify who to contact for a definition question (Steward) vs who approves access (Owner). |
| 2 | Four categories of data sources CPMAI expects you to map | Internal (warehouses/applications), External/third-party, Cloud repositories, Legacy archives. Exam tip: scenarios often combine 2-3 of these and ask which SME/steward to engage first. |
| 3 | Granularity rule for time-bound predictions (e.g., 30-day churn window) | Capture data at a granularity FINER than the prediction window (daily/transactional), never coarser (monthly). Coarse aggregation hides leading indicators. Exam tip: 'monthly summary' is a common wrong answer. |
| 4 | Sampling method for rare-event/imbalanced classes (fraud, churn, defects) | Stratified sampling with oversampling of the minority class (or undersampling majority). Simple random sampling underrepresents rare cases. Exam tip: 'simple random sampling' is the classic trap. |
| 5 | Validity (data quality dimension) — definition | Data conforms to a defined format, type, or allowable range (e.g., age 0-120). Distinct from Completeness (no missing values) and Timeliness (data is current). Exam tip: match the scenario keyword to the correct dimension name. |
| 6 | Six V's of Big Data tested in data-needs identification | Volume, Velocity, Variety, Veracity, Value, Variability. Exam tip: Veracity = trustworthiness/quality, often confused with Validity. |
| 7 | PMI-CPMAI exam weight for 'Identify Data Needs' domain | 26% of the exam — a heavily weighted domain covering data requirements, source mapping, and role identification. Exam tip: allocate proportional study time given its weight. |
| 8 | First step when a legacy/mainframe data source lacks documentation | Engage the SME who knows the legacy system to document field definitions BEFORE ingestion — never ingest raw legacy data blind. Exam tip: 'ingest directly' is always the trap answer. |
| 9 | When acquiring third-party/external data, what must be defined early? | Licensing terms, usage rights, and refresh/update cadence (a temporal requirement) — not just file format. Exam tip: overlooking licensing is a classic wrong-answer trap. |
| 10 | Change Data Capture (CDC) | Method to stream incremental changes from operational/transactional systems in near-real-time, used when batch warehouse loads are too slow (e.g., fraud detection). Exam tip: choose CDC over 'nightly batch' when the requirement says real-time. |
| 11 | Data Governance Team's role in the Identify Data Needs phase | Sets enterprise-wide policies, standards, and compliance rules (who can access what, retention rules) — distinct from the Steward (operational) and Owner (accountable authority). Exam tip: governance = policy-level, not day-to-day management. |
| 12 | Temporal requirements CPMAI expects you to define | Data freshness/latency needed (real-time vs. daily vs. monthly), historical depth required (e.g., years of history), and refresh frequency of external feeds. Exam tip: match data recency to the stated decision window in the scenario. |
