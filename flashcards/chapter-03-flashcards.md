## Chapter 3: AI Feasibility, Risk Assessment, and Scope Definition — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Three feasibility dimensions evaluated before AI project approval? | Technical (compute/infrastructure), Data (availability/quality/access), Organizational (skills/culture/change readiness). Exam tip: all three must pass — technically feasible but organizationally infeasible still blocks approval. |
| 2 | Three AI risk assessment categories tested on the exam? | Security (breach/unauthorized access/manipulation), Safety (physical/systemic harm), Ethics (bias, fairness, transparency). Exam tip: match scenario keywords — 'demographic disparity' = ethics, 'unauthorized access' = security, 'physical harm' = safety. |
| 3 | What must an AI project scope statement explicitly define regarding boundaries? | Specific, measurable in-scope and out-of-scope items — never vague goals. Exam tip: 'increase morale' or 'achieve business goals' are red-flag wrong-answer scope items. |
| 4 | What makes a valid 'success criteria' statement in an AI scope document? | It must be measurable/quantifiable (e.g., 'model accuracy ≥ 90%', 'reduce processing time by 30%'). Exam tip: vague statements like 'team is satisfied' are always wrong answers. |
| 5 | When should a project team recommend a traditional (non-AI) solution over an AI solution? | When business logic is deterministic/stable, data is insufficient, or added AI complexity produces no measurable ROI over a simpler system. Exam tip: honest comparison is tested — AI is not always the right answer. |
| 6 | AI vs traditional: fixed, rarely-changing business rules → which approach? | A deterministic rules engine/traditional automation. Exam tip: ML adds unnecessary maintenance overhead and reduces explainability for stable logic. |
| 7 | What does 'technical feasibility' assess in an AI project? | Whether required compute resources, infrastructure, integration capability, and technical skills exist to build/deploy the model. Exam tip: don't confuse with organizational feasibility (people/culture readiness). |
| 8 | What does 'data feasibility' assess? | Whether sufficient, high-quality, accessible, and properly governed data exists to train and support the AI solution. Exam tip: includes availability, quality, labeling, linkage/identifiers, and access rights. |
| 9 | What does 'organizational feasibility' assess? | Whether the organization has the skills, culture, change-management capacity, and stakeholder buy-in to adopt the AI solution. Exam tip: workforce fear of job loss is an organizational risk, not a technical one. |
| 10 | Example: security risk vs ethical risk in AI systems | Security risk: unauthorized access to model/data or adversarial manipulation. Ethical risk: biased/discriminatory outcomes across demographic groups. Exam tip: use scenario keywords to classify correctly. |
| 11 | Example of a safety risk in an AI system | Physical or systemic harm from AI failure — e.g., an autonomous vehicle misclassifying an obstacle, or a medical AI recommending an incorrect dosage. Exam tip: safety = harm to people/systems, distinct from ethics (fairness) and security (breach). |
| 12 | Why must scope statements explicitly list out-of-scope items? | To prevent scope creep and set clear stakeholder expectations about what the AI solution will NOT do. Exam tip: a mid-project request for unlisted functionality should go through change control, not be silently added. |
