## Chapter 1: Responsible AI Foundations: Privacy, Bias, and Governance — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | GDPR DPIA trigger threshold? | Required when processing is 'likely to result in high risk' — e.g., large-scale automated decision-making with legal/significant effects, systematic monitoring, or special category data (GDPR Art. 35). Exam tip: any AI system making automated decisions with legal effect on individuals almost always triggers a DPIA. |
| 2 | CCPA data disclosure look-back window? | 12 months — businesses must disclose personal info collected in the preceding 12 months on consumer request. Exam tip: contrast with GDPR, which has no fixed look-back period and applies to all processing regardless of date. |
| 3 | Maximum GDPR fine? | Up to €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever is HIGHER. Exam tip: exam may test knowing both figures and picking the greater one. |
| 4 | EEOC 'four-fifths rule' (disparate impact) threshold? | A protected group's selection rate below 80% of the highest-scoring group's rate signals potential adverse impact. Exam tip: given selection rates, compute the ratio and compare to 0.8. |
| 5 | Demographic parity vs. equalized odds? | Demographic parity = equal positive prediction rate across groups, regardless of true outcome. Equalized odds = equal TPR and FPR across groups, accounting for ground truth. Exam tip: equalized odds is the stricter, outcome-aware metric. |
| 6 | Pre-processing vs. in-processing vs. post-processing bias mitigation? | Pre-processing = modify training data before training (reweighting, resampling). In-processing = modify the algorithm/objective during training (fairness constraints, adversarial debiasing). Post-processing = adjust outputs/thresholds after training. Exam tip: match the scenario's lifecycle stage to the category. |
| 7 | SHAP vs. LIME? | SHAP = game-theoretic Shapley values, consistent and globally additive attributions. LIME = fits a simple local surrogate model around one prediction, faster but less stable. Exam tip: SHAP is preferred when consistency/theoretical guarantees matter. |
| 8 | What is a 'model card'? | Standardized documentation describing intended use, subgroup performance, limitations, training data, and ethical considerations of a model. Exam tip: model cards are the go-to artifact for transparency/accountability documentation questions. |
| 9 | What does a chain-of-custody record capture? | Who accessed or modified data/model artifacts at each lifecycle stage, with timestamps and approvals. Exam tip: tie chain-of-custody directly to reproducibility and audit readiness questions. |
| 10 | Data lineage vs. version control? | Data lineage = traceable record of a dataset's origin and transformations through the pipeline ('where from'). Version control = snapshot tracking of exact dataset/model/code states ('which exact copy'). Exam tip: full auditability needs BOTH together. |
| 11 | Encryption + access control layering for PII training data? | Encrypt at rest and in transit + apply RBAC/least privilege + maintain access logs. Exam tip: encryption alone satisfies confidentiality but NOT accountability — access logging is a separate required control. |
| 12 | Required elements of a Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA/DPIA)? | (1) Description of processing & purpose, (2) necessity/proportionality assessment, (3) risk to individuals' rights, (4) mitigation measures. Exam tip: scenario questions often ask which of these four elements is missing. |
